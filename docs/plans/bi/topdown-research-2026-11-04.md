# ReelBallers top-down market research (2026-11-04)

Scope: the TOP-DOWN market for a browser app where a parent uploads their own game film (phone, Veo, Trace, Hudl camera, any source), marks the plays, frames them, and the AI upscales them into sharp highlights and highlight reels. Multi-sport (soccer is about 75% of current users; product is for any filmed youth sport). Pricing today: pay only for the AI you use, no subscription. One Game $6.99 (about 10 plays), Popular $12.99 (about 24 plays), Season $29.99 (about 71 plays). 48 free credits at sign-up.

## Labeling conventions

- **B** = benchmark from a published source. Each B row names the source, the year the DATA refers to (not just the publication year), and a URL (full list in Sources at the end, numbered [n]).
- **A** = our own derivation. Each A row states the arithmetic. An A number is never a measurement.
- **B (secondary)** = the figure comes from a trade blog, aggregator or wiki draft rather than the primary publisher. Treat as weaker.
- US and non-US are kept in separate tables. Sport slices are kept in separate rows.
- "Competitive tier" below means an athlete currently on a travel, club, select or elite team, as opposed to a community, recreation or school-only team.
- No sport-season or dead-month assumptions are made anywhere. The one place a "two seasons a year" figure appears (section 6) is an explicit owner's guess, flagged as such, with no data behind it.

## 6. Top-down sizing (all A, arithmetic shown)

### 6.1 Chain and which factor has no published data

```
athletes in the competitive tier
  x share whose games are filmed in any form      <- NO PUBLISHED DATA (assumed)
  x annual spend at our price ladder              <- owner's guess (one or two Season packs a year)
  = serviceable addressable revenue per year
```

Factor with NO published data: the share of competitive-tier youth games (or athletes) that are filmed in any form. No Aspen, SFIA, AP-Ipsos or vendor source measures it. The low and high below bracket it at 30% to 60% for the US (reasoning: 22% of organizations livestream per a vendor survey [36]; Veo, Trace, Hudl Focus, Pixellot and phone filming add to that; club-tier teams are more likely than rec teams to be filmed; upper bound is a judgment). Treat the whole range as an assumption to be replaced by a measurement (for example, a one-question survey of sign-ups or of a club partner).

Spend assumption: low = one Season pack per athlete per year ($29.99); high = two Season packs per athlete per year ($59.98, owner's "two seasons a year" guess, no data). No assumption is made about when in the year those purchases happen.

### 6.5 What would tighten this most

1. One sign-up or in-app question: "Who filmed this game?" with phone / Veo / Trace / Hudl / other / club stream. That measures the filmed-share factor directly for our own traffic and removes the only no-data factor.
2. Veo or Trace disclosure of families per camera, or highlight downloads per match, would give a second filmed-share anchor; neither vendor publishes it (section 3.1).
3. Aspen's 2024 parent-survey microdata has spend by setting (travel/elite/club is a setting variable) but the published report does not cross-tabulate spend by setting; a data request to the Aspen / Utah State team would give the travel-tier spend directly.
